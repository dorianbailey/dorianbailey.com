What Is MetaZoo? The Trading Card Game Made of Cryptids

A card game built from cryptids, folklore and modern myths: where it came from, who makes it now, how a game plays and how the sets fit together.

By Dorian Bailey · November 13, 2026 · 4 minute read

MetaZoo is a trading card game made of cryptids: Mothman, the Loch Ness Monster, the Loveland Frogman and hundreds of other cards drawn from folklore and modern myth. It started in 2020, shut down in 2024 and returned in 2025 under a new publisher with rebuilt rules. Here is what MetaZoo is, who makes it, how a game plays and how the sets fit together.

What MetaZoo is: cryptids, folklore and the paranormal

Most card games invent their monsters. MetaZoo borrows them. Its creatures are the ones people have reported, feared and retold for generations: lake monsters, forest spirits, the thing on the back road that nobody quite saw. Encyclopedia summaries of the original game describe it as cryptozoology, folklore and the paranormal; the official site today calls its world cryptids, folklore and modern myths.

That is the hook, and it’s a good one. Every card points to a story you can go and read, and many of those stories belong to a real place. Point Pleasant, West Virginia, has Mothman. Pennsylvania has the Squonk. The Chickamauga battlefield in Georgia has Ole’ Green Eyes.

Who makes MetaZoo

The game began with MetaZoo Games, founded by Michael Waddell, which launched it in 2020 after a Kickstarter campaign during the pandemic. The first full set was Cryptid Nation. Collectors took to the cards quickly, and the DJ Steve Aoki became an equity partner.

On January 29, 2024, Waddell announced on the company’s Discord that MetaZoo Games was closing, effective immediately (ComicBook.com covered the shutdown). The rights were sold in bankruptcy court later that year, and in October 2024 a new team announced that the game would return.

That team is GameQbator Labs, which publishes MetaZoo today; MetaTwo Enterprises, the buyer in the bankruptcy sale, is the name on the official website. The people behind the relaunch are card-game veterans. The chief executive, Rick Arons, spent 21 years as an executive vice president of The Pokémon Company International. The lead game designer is Richard Garfield, who created Magic: The Gathering, with Skaff Elias as lead developer. The new Base Set reached stores on March 28, 2025, with more than 200 cards and all-new art.

How a game of MetaZoo plays

The relaunch kept the cryptids and rebuilt the game around them. Each player brings one Caster, a leader card that stays out of the main deck. Creatures are played by paying Aura, the game’s resource. Terra cards and lanes form the board, and creatures contest the lanes to score Mission Points.

In official play, you win by reaching at least 30 Mission Points and holding more than your opponent when a round ends. A constructed deck needs at least 50 cards in the main deck, with no more than three copies of any card.

The original game worked differently. Its creatures were called Beasties, players knocked down each other’s life points, and some cards asked for an action in the real world. If you find an older card in a binder, it comes from that first game and its rules.

How MetaZoo sets work

Since the relaunch, MetaZoo has followed a familiar pattern: a base set, then expansions that add creatures, Casters and new mechanics, each leaning on a type of Aura.

Booster boxes hold 24 packs, and a pack from the expansions holds 11 cards, one of them an Aura or token card. Around the main sets come preconstructed Flex Decks, which split into two half-decks so two people can learn together; Dynamic Decks, built for organized play; and limited releases such as Salem Nights, a Halloween release. As I write, the official site lists a premium set, Yokai Rising, as coming soon.

Where to start

  • Learn from the source. The official site has learn-to-play videos, the full rules and a card database you can sort by set.
  • Start with a deck. A preconstructed deck gets you playing the same day. Boosters can come once you know what you like.
  • Find a store. MetaZoo’s organized play runs from learn-to-play and casual nights to constructed events and store championships. If you’re thinking of competing, here is what to expect at your first MetaZoo tournament.
  • Collect by story. Begin with the creatures from places you know. A card means more when you can drive to the town its legend comes from.
